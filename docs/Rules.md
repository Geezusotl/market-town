# Prototype rules, fixed before engine implementation

Version 0.2. Closed accounting boundary, no banks, taxes, credit, external suppliers or new business entry. Not a predictive economic model.

Start with 60 households (each 10,000 cents), 2 input firms and 4 final goods firms (each 40,000 cents). Each firm has 10 fixed workers, who are also its equal owners. Input firms produce up to 30 units/day; final firms up to 15, consuming one input per final good. Each firm starts with 30 units of its output; final firms also start with 15 inputs. Each household needs one final good/day, consumed on purchase. Baseline prices: input 80 cents, final good 200 cents. Wage: 100 cents per employed worker/day. Every 7th day firms distribute cash above a 30 day wage reserve equally to owners. No magic replenishment. If a firm cannot afford a complete payroll it pays nobody, produces nothing, records the shortfall and closes after 7 consecutive such days; it remains closed with assets locked. Owners and workers are fixed in this prototype; hiring, liquidation and entry are deferred and visibly documented.

Tick order: scheduled capacity event; wages; input production; input market; final production; household market; owner distributions; price update; accounting checks. A firm with paid wages can buy input to support its actual workforce/capacity today. Sector prices are uniform, so pooled buyers are batch allocated available goods proportional to affordable demand. Largest remainder allocation handles indivisible units, with keyed seed/day/purpose/identity tie breaks. The seller side is allocated proportional to available stock independently. Sorted buyer and seller fills are then matched; no household gains priority merely by array position. All prices, cash, stock, counters and capacities are safe integers; intermediate products are checked. Fractional ratios are rounded explicitly using integer division. Sector prices rise by 5 cents after unmet affordable demand, fall by 5 when unused supply exceeds 10% of sold quantity, and otherwise hold. Bounds: input 40..300; final 120..600. Price is updated for next day. Lack of purchasing power is recorded separately from scarcity.

Shock: starting from day 30 checkpoint, halve input firm I00 capacity on days 31..60 inclusive; compare through days 90 and 365. Hold the day 30 prices fixed in a separate control for both branches. No change branches must match exactly. Small perturbation control reduces capacity by one unit on day 31 only. Seeds 0..29 are predetermined for paired comparisons. Scale 10 repeats the firm/household structure to 600 households and 60 firms.

Determinism: fixed rule version, fixed seed, no Date or Math.random inside engine. FNV-1a keyed deterministic tie ranking (not cryptographic or an assurance of perfect randomness). Stable decision keys, no global random cursor. Stable string ordering uses code unit comparisons, never localeCompare. UI time only affects playback. Checkpoints contain bounded recent events and 180 daily metrics; exported inputs and initial seed support deterministic replay. Links to events outside retained history are displayed as outside window. Event links describe direct dependencies/preconditions, not scientifically established causation.

Gate fixed before benchmarks: run release JS with 60 and 600 households, 5 repeats of 90 days after warmup. Baseline target >=20 days/second, desktop worker control acknowledgement <=100ms p95 under bounded step batches. Record browser/OS/CPU, startup, roundtrip, memory proxy and compressed static size. Local simulated devices do not establish real phone performance. Keep TypeScript if baseline meets target; profile before any rewrite. The static viewer is not an unattended server town.

Implementation self-review amendment: the initially specified final price floor of 100 was below full output unit cost at input price 40 (1,000 wages + 600 inputs over 15 goods). A floor of 120 allows 200 cents daily surplus per firm at full capacity. This correction is structural; no seeds were selected to achieve it. Shock failure remains a valid possible outcome and the fixed ownership/closure rules need independent scrutiny.

Revision 0.2: aggregate seed variation is not an uncertainty estimate; the one-unit perturbation is exactly 333 bps under floor rounding. Price feedback and no-entry closures remain unvalidated modeling assumptions. Day 365 and fixed-price control reporting are mandatory. The pooled market does not track physical lots; trade seller IDs are bookkeeping only. Market condition pointers do not establish unique causes. Supported checkpoint days are 0..100000 inclusive, with no step beyond the terminal day.
